Content plan for Lou, 90, the Brooklyn widower who was married 65 years. His niche is relationships and dating advice.

## The 8 ideas, ranked

**#1: Lou's Rules (series)**
- **Hook (first line he says):** "Lou's rule number one. If somebody only texts you after eleven o'clock... sweetheart, you're not a plan."
- **Format and hold:** a weekly rules series. Each episode is one rule plus the Angie story behind it, and it ends on a promise of the next rule.
- **Setting:** a 1970s Brooklyn living room, Lou in his brown leather armchair, warm lamp light, the phone propped on the side table.
- **Why it works:** "rules" series from older creators are a proven growth format. A named, numbered series gives people a reason to follow for the next one.

**#2: What's a situationship?**
- **Hook:** "A what-ship?"
- **Format and hold:** an interview / Q&A with Nicky off camera, playing on his hearing aid. It holds on his translation into 1955 language, then a one-line verdict.
- **Setting:** Sunday lunch, Lou at the family table with red sauce and rigatoni, Nicky filming across the table.
- **Why it works:** "asking my grandparent about modern dating words" is a reliably viral format. His bluntness about situationships hits the niche's biggest pain point right now.

**#3: Eleven cents**
- **Hook:** "I asked my wife out with eleven cents in my pocket."
- **Format and hold:** storytime. It holds on what eleven cents bought, and lands on the penny tip.
- **Setting:** a brownstone stoop at golden hour in autumn, Lou on the top step in a camel coat.
- **Why it works:** a specific, odd number in the first line. It's a love story with a payoff, the kind of content people save and send to someone.

**#4: Red flags from 1955 that still work**
- **Hook:** "Red flags from nineteen fifty-five that still work."
- **Format and hold:** signs and red flags, with a green-flag twist at the end.
- **Setting:** the living room armchair.
- **Why it works:** red-flag content is the backbone of the dating niche. Old-timer authority twists it into "timeless" advice.

**#5: If he wanted to, he'd call**
- **Hook:** "If he wanted to, he would. That's what you kids say, right? It's close."
- **Format and hold:** a contrarian twist on a viral phrase. It holds on his "fix" of the phrase and the story of the kitchen phone with the cord.
- **Setting:** Sunday lunch, Nicky handheld.
- **Why it works:** it rides a phrase the whole niche already uses and upgrades it into his catchphrase. That brands the account.

**#6: Rizz in 1955**
- **Hook:** "My what? Riz? Like rice?"
- **Format and hold:** an interview / comedy Q&A. It holds on what his "rizz" actually was.
- **Setting:** Sunday lunch.
- **Why it works:** Gen Z slang explained to a 90-year-old is a proven comedy format. The answer is genuinely sweet, which drives shares.

**#7: Ghosting**
- **Hook:** "Ghosting. Nicky explained it to me. I'm still upset."
- **Format and hold:** direct address. It holds on the bus-driver story and the one sentence you owe people.
- **Setting:** the stoop, Lou's own selfie held with both hands, too close.
- **Why it works:** ghosting is one of the niche's most-discussed frustrations. A moral stance from a grandpa feels like permission to do the decent thing.

**#8: The ring on the chain**
- **Hook:** "This is my wedding ring. It doesn't fit anymore, so it lives here."
- **Format and hold:** storytime with emotion. It holds on why the ring is on a chain, then the secret of 65 years.
- **Setting:** the armchair in the evening, a framed wedding photo on the side table.
- **Why it works:** the heart of the account, tied to his visual anchor. Long-marriage stories are among the most shared content in the niche.

**Shoot first:**
- **#1:** the series that makes people follow.
- **#2:** the most viral-shaped format; interview adds range.
- **#5:** turns the catchphrase into the brand before the product exists.

## Rules every script was checked against

- **Length:** 40 to 110 spoken words; mostly 71 to 80, which is about 30 seconds at Lou's slow pace.
- **Human-sounding:**
  - No balanced rule-of-three lists, and no thesis lines.
  - One self-interruption per script.
  - One uselessly specific detail (the court date, the comb "in case of wind", the sister's hip).
  - One near-repetition, and uneven rhythm.
- **Safety:**
  - Clarity and self-respect, never games.
  - Unsafe situations get a "you owe them nothing, block and go" note in the caption.
  - Advice works for any gender.
- **Before the store exists:** batch-1 CTAs never point to a store that doesn't exist yet.
