Card 20 of 25Index

I said something dumb.

The I-Was-Wrong Rule
“I was wrong. I’m sorry.” Then stop talking.

Sixty-five years of marriage, I said plenty of dumb things. The fix was never a big speech. It was “I was wrong, I’m sorry,” and then closing my mouth, which for me was always the hard part. A long sorry turns into a speech about you. A short one is about them.

Say this (on the phone)

Hi, it’s [your name]. I said something dumb the other night about [the thing].

It was thoughtless, and I’m sorry. You didn’t deserve that. That’s all. I just didn’t want it sitting there between us.

If you have to text
I said something dumb, and I want to apologize properly, not by text. Can I call you tonight?
Don’t say

“I’m sorry if you were offended.” That’s not a sorry. That’s a bill.

If they say no / don’t answer

If they’re not ready to hear it, leave it there. A sorry isn’t a ticket you get to cash in, so don’t keep apologizing until they forgive you. If they need time, give it.

If they’re done, they’re done, and you take the lesson to the next person: think one second longer before you talk. I’m still working on that one at ninety.

I sent a text I want back.

The Noon Rule
What you sent at midnight, you fix by noon.

My grandkids tell me people send things at two in the morning they’d never say at two in the afternoon. In my day the phone was on the kitchen wall, and my mother was in the kitchen. That kept things short. So: sleep, coffee, then one plain apology before lunch.

Say this (on the phone)

Hi, it’s [your name]. About the message I sent last night. That was too much, it was late, and I’m sorry.

You don’t need to answer it. I just wanted to say so out loud, instead of pretending it didn’t happen.

If you have to text
Last night’s message was too much. I’m sorry. Can I call you at lunch and say it properly?
Don’t say

“lol ignore that, I was so drunk” The drink didn’t hold the phone. You did.

If they say no / don’t answer

Then you let it rest. One apology, by noon, then hands off. No follow-up, no “Did you see my message?” If they write back kindly, lucky you.

If they don’t, take the lesson and charge your phone in the kitchen overnight for a while. Mine lived in the kitchen my whole life, and I never once sent anything I regretted at two in the morning.

I’m running late, or I have to cancel.

The Bus Driver’s Rule
Late is late. Don’t explain the traffic. Apologize for the wait.

Thirty-eight years I drove a city bus, and I can tell you, nobody waiting at a stop in the rain cares why you’re late. They care that somebody told them. So you call before the time, not after, and you give them the new time like you mean it.

Say this (on the phone)

Hi, it’s [your name]. I’m so sorry, I’m running about [twenty] minutes late. That’s on me. I’ll be there at [7:20]. If that wrecks your night, we can move it.

If you have to cancel: I have to cancel tonight, and I’m sorry. I know you planned around it. Can I take you out [Thursday] at [seven] instead? My treat.

If you have to text
Running [20] minutes late, my fault, so sorry. I’ll be there by [7:20]. Still okay, or should we move it to [Thursday]?
Don’t say

“Traffic was crazy.” Ten minutes after you were due. That’s a weather report, not an apology.

If they say no / don’t answer

If they’re upset, let them be upset. You earned it. Say “I understand, and I’m sorry,” and don’t argue your case.

If they’d rather call the night off, accept it without a fuss and offer one new day. And next time, leave when you think you’re early. That’s what on time looks like.

6They crossed a line
Card 23 of 25Index

They said something that hurt me.

The Ouch Rule
If it stung, say “ouch” out loud. What they do next is your answer.

Everybody says a dumb thing sometimes. I’ve said a few hundred. So the first time, you figure it was dumb, not mean, and you tell them. Not in a text with three paragraphs. Out loud, once, plain. It’s never the dumb thing that does the damage. It’s the dumb thing nobody mentions, sitting there for a year.

Say this (on the phone)

Can I tell you something? Wait. When you said [what they said], it stung.

I don’t think you meant it that way, but I wanted you to know, because I’d rather tell you than sit on it.

If you have to text
Something from the other night is still bugging me, and I’d rather say it than text it. Can I call you tonight at [eight]?
Don’t say

“It’s fine.” When it isn’t. “It’s fine” is how small things get big.

If they say no / don’t answer

A good person says “I’m sorry, I didn’t think.” A not-so-good one tells you you’re too sensitive. Now you know which one you’ve got.

If it keeps happening after you said it plain, it’s not a slip anymore, it’s a habit, and you’re allowed to leave (card 18 (p. ) or card 19 (p. )). If what they say makes you feel scared or small all the time, go to card 25 (p. ).

6They crossed a line
Card 24 of 25Index

They keep pushing after I said no.

The One-Word Rule
No is one word. It doesn’t need an explanation, and it doesn’t get a recount.

Pushing comes in a lot of flavors: “come on,” “just one more drink,” “you said maybe last time,” “if you really liked me.” Doesn’t matter the flavor. You said no, to coming over, to moving faster, to whatever it was, and that’s the whole conversation. Anybody worth your time hears it the first time.

Say this (on the phone)

I want to say something clearly, so there’s no confusion. Slow down. Say it like a fact, because it is one.

I said no to [staying over / moving faster / that], and I meant it. I’m not going to keep explaining. If we’re going to keep seeing each other, I need you to stop asking.

If you have to text
I said no, and I meant it. Please don’t ask again.
Don’t say

“Maybe another time.” If you mean no. A soft no just gets asked again.

If they say no / don’t answer

If they argue, sulk or ask again, that’s your answer about them. You don’t owe a second no, but if you want one: “I already answered.” Then end the call.

If they don’t stop, if you feel scared, or if they push with their hands instead of their words, go to card 25 (p. ) right now. That isn’t dating anymore.

If they scare you, this isn’t a dating problem.

The Bravest Call Rule
The bravest call in this whole file isn’t to them.

Listen to me, sweetheart. If you’re scared of how they’ll react, if they check your phone, decide who you can see, control your money, threaten you or hurt you, no card in here is for that. That’s when you call somebody whose whole job is to help. It isn’t weak. It’s the strongest call you’ll ever make.

Say this (on the phone)

To a helpline. It’s free and private, and somebody answers day and night.

Hi. I’m not sure if this counts, but I’m scared of the person I’m with. [One thing that happened.] I don’t know what to do next.

US: National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233

UK: Refuge, National Domestic Abuse Helpline, 0808 2000 247

In danger right now: 911 in the US, 999 in the UK, or your local emergency number.

If you have to text

If calling isn’t safe, the US hotline takes texts: text START to 88788. Or send one person you trust:

Can you call me? I’m not okay, and I need to talk.
Don’t say

“Maybe I’m overreacting.” If you’re scared, you’re not overreacting. It counts.

If they say no / don’t answer

If the line is busy, try again, or use the text line. If you think they check your phone, call from a friend’s phone or a work phone.

Don’t use the breakup cards in this file on someone who scares you. The people on those lines can help you plan how to leave safely. And you call them. I mean it.
