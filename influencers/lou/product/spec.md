Digital product: Lou, 90, relationships and dating (clarity, courage, manners, self-respect).

**Status: built.** The PDF has 40 pages, as planned, with 25 cards. It was audited and verified; see `audit.md`. ## Title, subtitle, byline

- **Title:** Don't Text. Call.
- **Subtitle:** Grandpa Lou's swipe file for the moments you don't know what to say
- **Byline (cover):** Grandpa Lou, 90, married 65 years
- **Running footer (every page except the cover):** DON'T TEXT. CALL. · GRANDPA LOU, plus the page number

## Cover promise line

The test: would this line alone make the buyer want it? It uses the real card count (25).

1. **"Word-for-word scripts for the 25 moments that keep you up at night"** (PICKED)
2. "25 situations. The exact words. And what to do if they say no."
3. "What to say when you're scared to ask, when they go quiet, and when it's time to go"

Option 1 wins. In ten words it names the format (word-for-word scripts), the size (25) and the exact pain (lying awake at 11pm with the phone in your hand). Option 2 is punchy, but "situations" is cold. Option 3 is lovely but vague: it doesn't say what you get.

## Catchphrase placement

"Don't text. Call." is the title, so it is on the cover by definition. Inside, it appears at exactly two designed moments:
1. The sign-off of the letter from Lou (page 3).
2. The closing line of the small print, the last page.

It is not used anywhere else in the body copy. The running footer carries the title, identically on every page, so it reads as a footer, not as a flourish.

## Buyer and price

- **Buyer:** 18 to 35, any gender and orientation, dating in the app-and-text era. They hit "buy" in the moment: phone in hand, a message typed eleven times, or a silence they can't read. They also buy in calm moments, when they want to finally ask someone out.
- **Price:** $12 launch, regular $15.99 shown crossed out. The justification is 25 situation cards with word-for-word scripts, a usage log, a 5-part bonus stack and a 40-page designed PDF.

## Shape

Situation-indexed cards, one card per page, identical structure, navigable BY FEELING. The contents page ("Find your feeling") groups the cards by how the reader feels, not by what happened. Every card name is a link in the PDF, and every card links back to the index.

### Card structure (identical on all 25 cards)

1. **Header:** feeling group (gold ring numeral + group name), "Card N of 25", and a link back to the index.
2. **Situation title.** The title IS the situation, in the buyer's own words.
3. **Lou's take, as a named rule:** the rule name plus a one-line take in Lou's voice. Under it, a short note (about 45 to 75 words) in Lou's voice that carries one specific detail from his world.
4. **"Say this (on the phone)":** word-for-word, in the BUYER's natural voice, with [brackets] for what they fill in. Written to be said aloud; it works face to face as well.
5. **"If you have to text":** one short message. Its only job is to set up a call or a clear plan with a day and a time. On the "say no" cards, where a call could feel imposing, its job is a clear, kind answer. It never holds the conversation by text.
6. **"Don't say":** one line, struck through, plus a few words on why.
7. **"If they say no / don't answer":** what to do next. It always respects their answer and never pushes past a no.

### The 25 cards and their named rules

Lines marked (V) are verbatim alignment lines from the coordinator. Lou's Instagram scripts quote them word for word, so they must appear exactly as given. Only light polish goes around them.

3. They said yes to a call. Now I have to dial. **The Reason Rule:** "You don't need a speech, you need a reason." (V) Opens with: "Hi, it's [your name]. Is now a good time? I was thinking about Saturday and I wanted to hear your voice." (V) Bad time: "No problem, when's better?" (V)

**2. They went quiet**

## Measurement loop (reactive version)

There is no score. The buyer keeps a usage log instead.
- **Opening half (page 5):** "The conversation I've been putting off" and the date, written in.
- **The log (pages 33-34):** date, card used, what happened, how I felt (before > after), and a drawn "called" tick box.
- **Closing half (page 34):** "Read it back": count the ticks, read the "after" column, go back to page 5 and tick whether that conversation happened.

## Safety decisions

- No manipulation, games, jealousy tactics or "make them want you". It is stated plainly (letter, Why this works, small print): **no script makes anyone want you.**
- Every "if they say no" branch accepts the no. Card 24 is about the buyer's own no being pushed; Card 25 is the dedicated safety card.
- **Card 25** names real free help and frames calling as the strong move:
  - US: National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233 (text START to 88788 if calling isn't safe)
  - UK: Refuge, National Domestic Abuse Helpline, 0808 2000 247
  - Elsewhere: the local emergency number (911 US, 999 UK)
  - Practical safety: call from a phone they can't check, and don't use the breakup cards on someone who scares you.
- Small print adds crisis lines for buyers in a dark place: US 988 (call or text), UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123.
- Anxiety adaptation: practical habits only, plus "a doctor or a counselor is the strong move". No treatment claims.
- AI disclosure, said plainly and warmly in the small print: Lou is an AI-created fictional character.
