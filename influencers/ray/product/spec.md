# The Quiet Money Workbook: design spec

Digital product: Ray, 71, retired plumber (money: frugal habits, never financial advice).

This spec was locked before any page copy was written. The method normally stops twice for the user: once to pick the idea and once to approve the sample. The owner has handed everything over, so both decisions were made here and are recorded below.

## Decisions taken in place of the "STOP and ask" moments

| Moment | Decision | By |
|---|---|---|
| Idea pick (Phase 1) | **Reckoning + ritual workbook.** The buyer is avoiding a shameful money mess: unopened statements, forgotten subscriptions, "where did it all go?" every month, a knot in the stomach when the bank app opens. A shame-avoider won't read scripts and won't do a 30-day plan. They need one guided honest sitting, then a short ritual they keep. | Coordinator, confirmed against the skill's moment table |
| Sample approval (Phase 2b) | One Leak Hunt step (Step 4, The Drips) is written and built at full quality, rendered, reviewed against the checklist below, fixed and locked. The first Rule page and the first Sunday Sums page are then reviewed as type samples before their batches, because the book has three different unit shapes. | Self-approved; the review notes are in the "Sample record" section at the end of this file |
| Page count | **48 pages**, above the brief's typical 30 to 45. Twelve of them are identical weekly Sunday Sums pages, which the ritual needs, and eight are the Leak Hunt fill-in pages. Every page is a working page or a front-matter page the skill requires. Nothing is padded; there is no bare divider page. | Self |

## Title, subtitle, promise

- **Title:** The Quiet Money Workbook
- **Subtitle:** Ray's one honest sitting and a fifteen-minute Sunday ritual
- **Byline:** Ray, 71, retired plumber
- **Catchphrase:** "Rich is quiet." It appears on the cover and at exactly two designed moments inside: the sign-off of the letter from Ray (page 3) and the close of "Twelve Sundays On" (page 42). It appears nowhere else.

### Cover promise line: three options

1. **"Find the leaks in one sitting. Then open the bank app without the knot in your stomach."** (CHOSEN)
2. "Find out where it all goes in one honest sitting, then keep it calm in fifteen minutes a week."
3. "Open the envelopes, find the drips, and stop asking where it all went every month."

**Why option 1:** it names the exact physical moment of the pain (opening the bank app, the knot in the stomach) and uses Ray's own metaphor (leaks). It gives a concrete "how" (one sitting), and it promises calm, not money. Option 2 mostly repeats the subtitle, which already says "one sitting" and "fifteen minutes", so it wastes the cover's second line. Option 3 is picturable, but "stop asking" claims more than a workbook can guarantee. The test was whether the line alone would make the buyer want it. Option 1 is the only one that describes the buyer's own Sunday-night feeling. There is no savings figure anywhere on the cover or inside.

## Buyer and price

- **Buyer:** someone with a money mess they don't look at. It is not always a crisis: often it's a steady fog of forgotten subscriptions, small daily spends and statements they don't open. They buy late at night, after an end-of-month "where did it all go?", or just after a bank app notification they didn't want to open.
- **Price:** $12 launch price, regular $15.99 shown crossed out. Link-in-bio store (Stan Store or Payhip).

## Content alignment with Ray's Instagram scripts (locked, do not paraphrase)

1. **Leak Hunt:** one sitting, kettle on. Every statement, every little drip, and next to every outgoing ONE question: **"Would I buy this again today?"** (not "do I need it"). The plumber framing, verbatim: **"When I find a leak, I don't start fixing. I turn the water off and I look."** In the book, that question is a Yes/No column on every outgoings table (Steps 3, 4 and 5), and Step 6 (The No Pile) turns every No into an action.
2. **The Thirty-Day Wait** (Rule 4): anything over **fifty quid** that's a want, not a need, goes on a list **pinned to the shed door** and waits **thirty days**. If he still wants it after thirty days, he buys it, **no guilt, no fuss**. Story: **a pressure washer went on the list in 2012**. He still hasn't got one, and he's still got a bucket.
3. **Sunday Sums:** every Sunday after his dinner, a brew and the notebook, fifteen minutes. **"Last week's money, then next week's money."** Maureen does the shopping column. The weekly page is built in two halves: last week's money (came in, went out, in minus out), then next week's money (what's coming up). Small extras: three checks, a calm score, a "What I noticed:" line and the done tick-box. It still fits in fifteen minutes.
4. **Script details echoed in the book:**
   - "the forty little things you don't write down" (Step 5)
   - "skint isn't a character flaw, it's a maths problem" (the letter)
   - the March 1987 pasties entry, 2.40, "hungry? No. Bored." (Step 5, and shown in the Bonus 5 artefact)
   - Christmas 1991: a bike for Gary, sixty-two pounds, ten pound a month in an envelope since June, with a little star in the margin, set against the Christmas before on the never-never and still paying in March (Rule 7)
   - "I am the warranty" (Bonus 4)
   - the 2004 van, 191,000 miles, "She's not pretty. She's paid for." (Rule 3)

## Unit structures (identical within each type)

**Leak Hunt step:** a header strip ("The Leak Hunt" plus "Step N of 8") with an eight-segment progress bar; the title; a one-line deck; Ray's paragraph in voice with one specific detail; "Do this" as short numbered instructions; the fill-in (table or lines; outgoings tables carry the "Would I buy this again today?" Yes/No column); a "What I noticed:" line at the foot.

**Rule page:** a header strip ("Ray's Rules" plus "Rule N of 7") with a seven-segment progress bar; the rule's name; the rule in one sentence (large italic); "The story" (from the details bank); "How it works" (practical, a varied number of points); "The honest bit" (where the rule doesn't fit, added during the build); "Try it this week" (fill-in lines plus a tick-box).

**Sunday Sums week:** a header strip ("Sunday Sums" plus "Week N of 12") with a twelve-segment progress bar; the date line and a kettle-on tick-box; **Last week's money** (came in; went out by row: pipework, the shopping, getting about, small leaks, what's owed, the future, the Friday Fish, anything else; total out; in minus out); **Next week's money** (coming up: what, which day, how much; next week's Friday Fish); three checks as tick-boxes (every envelope opened, new drips written down, Shed Door List looked at); calm this week out of 10; "What I noticed:"; the "Sunday Sums done" tick-box.

- **Type:** Young Serif (titles, display; warm, a little 1970s British) and Source Serif 4 (body, labels, tables). Handwriting artefacts only: Reenie Beanie (Ray's 2026 worked example) and Zeyada (the 1987 notebook). All four are loaded from local files in `build/fonts/`.
- **Page furniture:** A4, a thin navy frame inset 8 mm with a fine pencil inner rule, and generous margins (about 18 mm). The running footer reads "The Quiet Money Workbook · Ray" with the page number, on every page except the cover.
- **Fill-ins:** ledger-style tables (cream header, pencil hairlines, amount columns right-aligned), fill-in lines with handwriting room (about 8 to 9 mm), and tick-boxes drawn as bordered CSS cells. No unicode ticks, arrows or boxes anywhere.
- **Money columns:** headed "Amount", not "£", so the book works for dollar readers too. Ray's own examples are in pounds.

## Safety decisions

- Money stays at principles, organisation and habits. No investment advice, no product or provider names (except the free help organisations), no promised returns, no savings figures. "Pay the Future First" is a principle about the order money goes out. What "the future" means is the reader's call, and Ray says plainly that where to put it is not his trade.
- On debt order, both schools are named and the reader picks. Priority debts (home, heating, anything heading to court) come first, as the free debt charities advise.
- Serious cases go to free, impartial debt help, framed as the strong move ("a good plumber doesn't fix a burst main on his own"). Never a lender.
- Couples: an economic-abuse box says plainly that controlled money is not a budgeting problem, and names real helplines (UK National Domestic Abuse Helpline 0808 2000 247; US National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233).
- Small print: Ray is an AI-created fictional character. The book is general information, not financial, debt or other advice. It names free help, plus Samaritans 116 123 (UK and ROI) and 988 (US) for anyone not coping.
- No invented statistics. Research is hedged ("research on habits keeps finding...", "one well-known study found most people took around two months..."). The ostrich effect is named as a real concept with no figures attached.
- No em dashes or en dashes; no unicode arrows, ticks or boxes; no brands.

## Sample record (Phase 2b)

**Sample unit:** Leak Hunt Step 4, The Drips, built alone as `build/out/sample.pdf` and reviewed at 110 dpi and at 220 dpi.

First render, problems found:
- The page ran about 13 mm over: the "What I noticed" lines fell below the footer, into the frame.
- The "What I noticed:" label and its first line didn't sit on one row, because of a CSS `:first-of-type` selector bug.
- The total row drew empty bordered cells under "Last used" and the Yes/No columns.
- The table caption didn't show the actual question.

Fixes:
- Row height 8.1 mm to 7.6 mm, which is still enough to handwrite in.
- The noticed block rebuilt as label plus line on one row, then a second full line.
- Tightened heading margins.
- The total row now shows only the label and the one cell that takes a written total.
- The caption now reads "Every drip, and the one question next to it: *Would I buy this again today?*"

Second render: it fits, with no overflow, and no glyph or header-wrap problems.

**Locked by the sample:**
- **Voice:** an opener tic ("Right."), one true detail from the bank ("about four, and every one of them is in a notebook"), shame removed ("that's not daft, it's design") and one plumbing image carried through (the bucket under the drip). Two short paragraphs; no lecture.
- **Weight:** two paragraphs of voice, four numbered instructions, one ledger fill-in, one small tally line, and "What I noticed:".
- **Structure:** the Leak Hunt step template described above. Every outgoings table carries the Again column.
- **Facts:** only plain, checkable statements ("some things only charge once a year"). No numbers that aren't the reader's own.

## Build notes (decisions made after the sample)

- **Rule pages gained "The honest bit".** Every rule page was left with 40 to 60 mm of dead space. Rather than stretch the type, each rule got a fifth element, "The honest bit", which says plainly where that rule doesn't fit (for example "Don't mend what can hurt you", or "Cheap isn't always cheaper"). It is the same element on all seven pages.
- **"Very little coming in" moved from page 9 to page 8** to balance the two Make It Fit pages. Page 9 gained an interactive choice: "My school" with two tick-boxes, plus a line for any priority debts.
- **The midpoint letter sits after Week 3**, not after Week 6. The book's own "What to Expect" page names week three as the dip, so the letter goes where motivation actually dies.
- **The weekly page's "Came in" accepts "what landed, or a week's share"**, so monthly-paid and four-weekly-paid readers aren't left with a meaningless weekly sum. The ritual page explains both options.
- **Final page count: 48**, as planned. Contents numbers are generated from the real page order and checked against the PDF text by `build/verify.py`.

## Source map

- `build/build.js` assembles the HTML, numbers the pages, fills the contents, checks every page for overflow and low fill, and prints the PDF.
- `build/verify.py` checks every unit's title against its real PDF page and every contents number against the real page. It also confirms there are no unresolved references and no banned characters.
- `build/src/*.js` holds the content in batches: front matter, Leak Hunt, Rules, Sunday Sums, and the back.
- `build/src/styles.css` holds the palette and typography.
- `build/fonts/` holds the local font files (Young Serif, Source Serif 4, Reenie Beanie, Zeyada).
- `build/mockups/render.js` renders the two store images.
