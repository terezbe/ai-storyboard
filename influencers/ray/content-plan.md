# Content plan: Ray, 71, money and frugal living

> **מה יש כאן:** 8 רעיונות מדורגים ו-15 תסריטים מוכנים להקלטה.
> - **10 תסריטים לגל הראשון.** עולים ביום 4, כולם באותו יום.
> - **5 תסריטים לגל השני.** מוכוונים למוצר, יוצאים מיום 5 אם ריי מנצח.
>
> **איך משתמשים:**
> - את ה-Script מדביקים כמו שהוא בכלי הקול.
> - את ה-On-screen text שמים על הווידאו בעריכה.
> - את ה-Post caption מדביקים בתיאור הפוסט.
> - הפרומפטים לווידאו נמצאים ב-`video-prompts.md`.

Ray, 71, is the retired Yorkshire plumber who paid off his house at 41. His niche is money and frugal living.

## The 8 ideas, ranked

**#1: Red flags your money has a leak**
- **Hook (first line he says):** "Red flags your money's got a leak. From a plumber."
- **Format & hold:** signs and red flags, a plumber's diagnosis. The last "flag" turns out to be the whole flood.
- **Setting:** a small Formica kitchen table, a brown teapot, an open notebook and an old calculator, net curtains, grey daylight.
- **Why it works:** red-flag lists are a proven format. Money told through plumbing is a USP nobody else in finance can copy, and it leads straight into the workbook's Leak Hunt.

**#2: Ray's Rules: things I've never paid for (series)**
- **Hook:** "Things I have never paid for in seventy-one years."
- **Format & hold:** a listicle, as a weekly series. Every item is oddly specific, and the last one lands.
- **Setting:** the kitchen table, Ray with a brew, the phone propped against the teapot.
- **Why it works:** "things I refuse to pay for" lists are big in frugal living. A 71-year-old tradesman makes each item a credible flex, and the format repeats forever.

**#3: You lot call it loud budgeting**
- **Hook:** "You lot call it loud budgeting. We called it no."
- **Format & hold:** a contrarian take on a trend. It holds on how "no" works in practice and on the punchline.
- **Setting:** the shed, Ray at the workbench with jam jars of screws, soft window light.
- **Why it works:** it rides the loud-budgeting and quiet-saving trend. An old man treating the trend as nothing new is a proven comedy angle.

**#4: How I paid off my house at forty-one**
- **Hook:** "Nineteen seventy-nine, we bought it for fourteen and a half thousand."
- **Format & hold:** an interview with Kelly off camera. It holds on "how", and the answer is boring. That's the point.
- **Setting:** the kitchen table, Ray answering his granddaughter off camera, never looking at the lens.
- **Why it works:** a specific, aspirational number from an unlikely person. The answer is principles, not products, so it's safe and highly saveable.

**#5: The Notebook (series)**
- **Hook:** "March nineteen eighty-seven. Two pounds forty. On a pasty I didn't even want."
- **Format & hold:** storytime from his notebooks, one real entry per episode. It holds on what the entry taught him.
- **Setting:** the kitchen table, Ray reading from an old spiral notebook.
- **Why it works:** a signature series with a built-in prop and endless material (forty-one notebooks). Each episode is a tiny storytime with a real lesson.

**#6: My van is twenty-two years old**
- **Hook:** "My van is twenty-two years old, and people keep asking when I'll get a new one."
- **Format & hold:** storytime with a contrarian edge. It holds on his answer.
- **Setting:** the cab of an old white van on a terraced street, rain on the windscreen, Ray in the driver's seat.
- **Why it works:** car flexing is the opposite of his brand. An honest, funny "keep what works" lands with anyone who has a car payment.

**#7: The richest man on my street**
- **Hook:** "The richest man on my street drives a two thousand and four van."
- **Format & hold:** storytime with a reveal. He describes a neighbour, and the twist is that it's him. "Rich is quiet."
- **Setting:** the van cab, parked.
- **Why it works:** a curiosity gap plus a reveal. It's the catchphrase in story form, and the last line gets screenshotted.

**#8: If you're twenty-five and skint**
- **Hook:** "If you're twenty-five and skint, listen to an old man for thirty seconds."
- **Format & hold:** POV direct address. It holds on the one thing he'd do first, then a soft plug for the workbook in batch 2.
- **Setting:** the kitchen table, the phone propped against the biscuit tin.
- **Why it works:** it calls out the niche's biggest audience segment directly, from "I was skint too", and leads to the product.

**Shoot first:** #1 (monetisation lead-in and the USP), #2 (the series opener) and #4 (the big aspirational number; the interview format adds range).

## Rules every script was checked against

- **Length:** 40 to 110 spoken words; most scripts land at 67 to 82, so each fits a 30-second generation at his pace.
- **Sounding human:**
  - No balanced rule-of-three lists and no thesis lines.
  - One self-interruption per script.
  - One uselessly specific detail per script (the chicken scissors, the pressure washer, the heater stuck on full).
  - One near-repetition per script, and uneven rhythm.
- **Safety:**
  - Money is principles and habits only: no investment, product or provider advice, and no promised savings.
  - Y4 is a story, not instructions.
- **Calls to action:** batch-1 scripts don't point to a store that doesn't exist yet.
