# Spec: The 30-Day Morning Reset

Digital product: Rosa, 94, healthy aging and old ways (`influencers/rosa/`).

The owner delegated every decision, so there was no human pick and no human sample approval. This file records each decision in place of those two moments.

## Coordinator locks (to match Rosa's Instagram scripts)

- **Day 1:** before the phone, go to the window, open it WIDE (not a crack) and stand there one minute with light on your face, looking at whatever is outside. Rosa's line appears verbatim: "You don't need anything, just a window."
- **Day 2:** a BIG glass of water (not a little one) before the coffee.
- **Day 10 (movement week):** walk to the shop or bakery yourself for something small, instead of driving or ordering it.
- **Themes used:**
  - Sitting down to eat, never standing over the sink "like a thief": Day 15.
  - The first conversation of the day with a person, not a screen: Day 22.
  - Riposo, a short afternoon rest of about forty minutes, framed as rest and not as a health claim: Day 24.
  - An evening walk: Day 25.
  - Sunday lunch with people: Day 21.

## Cover promise line

The test is whether this line alone would make the buyer want the book.

1. "30 small mornings to wake up with more energy". It is concrete and honest, but it is also the line every wellness product uses, so it does not stop the scroll.
2. **"One small habit a day to stop feeling old before 9 a.m."** It names the buyer's exact pain with a time stamp, and it tells them the mechanism: one small habit a day. Next to a 94-year-old face it carries a wink: she isn't old at nine, so why are you? It is a lifestyle feeling, not a health claim.
3. "Thirty mornings to go from grabbing the phone to breakfast sitting down". It is very picturable, but it describes the behavior rather than the feeling people pay for.

**Pick: #2.** The word "old" recurs on the cover: in the subtitle ("old ways"), in the promise ("feeling old") and in the catchphrase ("how old you are"). That is deliberate. Old ways answer the feeling of being old, and the sea doesn't care about age.

## Buyer

- **Who:** an adult, roughly 30 to 65, whose mornings start with a phone in the face, coffee standing at the sink, no real breakfast, and a body that feels heavy by nine. Many are parents, office or shift workers, or people living alone.
- **Moment of purchase:** scrolling in the evening or on a sluggish morning, seeing a 94-year-old who swims at dawn, and thinking "she has more go in her than I do."

## Lifestyle twist (the opening argument)

People see Rosa in the sea at 94 and say "lucky genes, lucky sea." The letter turns that around: the swim is the *receipt* for 85 years of small, ordinary habits, not the secret. None of those habits needs a sea. The swim stays her story and is never the buyer's task. Every mention carries her safety line: only when the sea is kind, never alone, and on rough days she stays by the ladder.

### The named rules (the product's IP)

**Week One: Light and water.** The first ten minutes.

1. The Window Rule: before the telephone, the window, wide open. *(locked)*
2. The Big Glass Rule: a big glass of water before the coffee. *(locked)*
3. The Wrist Rule: cool tap water on the face and wrists. This is the safe swap for her sea.
4. The Doorstep Rule: outside within the first hour, for five minutes.
5. The Basil Rule: give something alive a drink. A reason to go to the window.
6. The Hallway Rule: the telephone sleeps outside the bedroom.
7. The Bell Rule: one wake-up time every day, Sunday too.

**Week Two: Gentle movement.** The 112 steps. Every day has a seated or smaller version.

8. The Counting Rule: one flight of stairs on purpose, counted, with a hand on the rail.
9. The Cat Rule: stretch for one minute before you stand.
10. The Bread Rule: walk to the shop or bakery for something small. *(locked)*
11. The Tall Rule: back to the wall, thirty seconds.
12. The Heron Rule: balance at the counter while the coffee brews, with both hands on the counter.
13. The Stool Rule: stand up and sit down slowly, five times, from a sturdy chair.
14. The Broom Rule: one household job by hand, done properly.

**Halfway letter** (between Days 14 and 15).

**Week Three: Eating like Nonna.** This is about *how* to eat, never *what*. No diet prescriptions.

15. The Chair Rule: never eat standing over the sink "like a thief".
16. The Moka Rule: set the morning up the night before.
17. The Table Rule: no telephone at the table.
18. The Market Rule: buy one thing you have to wash or peel.
19. The One Pot Rule: cook one simple thing from scratch.
20. The One O'Clock Rule: lunch is a meal, not a pause.
21. The Sunday Table Rule: one meal with people around you.

**Week Four: People and rest.** A morning is made the day before.

22. The Good Morning Rule: your first conversation of the day is with a person, not a screen.
23. The Concetta Rule: five real minutes with a neighbor.
24. The Riposo Rule: rest after lunch (hers is about forty minutes).
25. The Evening Walk Rule: a slow walk after supper.
26. The Voice Rule: call, don't type.
27. The Octopus Rule: when the morning goes wrong, laugh first.
28. The Last Boat Rule: when the last boat is in, the day is done (bedtime).

**The last two mornings**

29. The Whole Morning Rule: your favorite rules in one morning, in your order.
30. The Three Words Rule: never say "at my age".

## Unit structure (identical on every day page)

1. Top band: week label (left), "Day N of 30" (right), and a 30-dot progress bar with today's dot in coral.
2. The named rule (large display serif), then the rule in one italic line.
3. Rosa's story or teaching in her voice. It runs 130 to 190 words and uses one specific detail from her bible.
4. The task box: a drawn tick-box, "Today", the tiny task in one or two sentences, then "Too much today?" with a smaller version.
5. "What I noticed:" with at least three ruled lines, extended to fill the page.
6. A running footer, "THE 30-DAY MORNING RESET · NONNA ROSA", and the page number.

## Visual identity

- **Palette** (from her wardrobe and world):
  - sea turquoise `#1F8C8F` (tint `#DDEFEC`)
  - coral red from her beads `#C8463A`
  - linen cream from her shirt `#FBF6EC` (pages), `#F3EADA` (cover and dividers)
  - navy from her trousers and Sunday dress `#1E2C47` (text)
  - a touch of lemon yellow from the bowl on her table `#F2CC55` (tint `#FBF0CC`)
- **Type:** Fraunces for display (soft, slightly wonky old-style serif: mischievous, warm) and Lora for body (warm, readable book serif). Both are local TTFs, so nothing depends on system fonts.
- **Numerals:** small navigation numbers (day numbers, page numbers, the contents, week maps, the Fridge Sheet) are set in Lora. Fraunces' flat-topped 3 reads like a 5 at small sizes.
- **Write-in space:** every day page has at least three "What I noticed" lines. The build then extends the ruled lines into whatever space the page has left, so every day page ends on the same baseline.
- **Page furniture:**
  - a thin navy frame inset on every interior page
  - a running footer, "THE 30-DAY MORNING RESET · NONNA ROSA", with the page number on every page except the cover
  - drawn CSS tick-boxes and ruled fill-in lines
  - no unicode ticks, arrows or boxes
- **Images:**
  - the cover portrait (`profile-picture.jpg`) in an arch window frame
  - the four week openers use arch panels cropped from her three location stills:
    - Week 1: kitchen window
    - Week 2: doorstep alley
    - Week 3: kitchen table and chair
    - Week 4: boats at dusk
  - the midpoint letter uses the ladder at the rocks, her story
  - a small round portrait on the opening letter

## Sample unit (Phase 2b): Day 1, written first to finished quality

> **Day 1 of 30 · Week One: Light and water**
> **The Window Rule.** *Before the telephone, the window. Wide open.*
>
> The first thing I touch every morning is the window. Not the moka, not the telephone: the window. I open it wide, both sides, all the way, and I stand there with the light on my face and look. The sea tells me what kind of day it is. Flat. Angry. Or that silver color it goes in February, when it's thinking about something.
>
> Giulia's telephone sleeps next to her pillow like a cat. The first thing she sees every morning is a little glass box full of other people's news. Then she comes into my kitchen with a face like yesterday's bread and asks me why she's tired.
>
> Listen to me. Your eyes wake up before the rest of you. Give them the sky first, not the box. One minute. You don't need a sea, amore. You don't need anything, just a window. A parking lot has a sky over it too.
>
> **[ ] Today:** Before you touch the telephone, go to a window and open it wide, not a crack. Stand there for one whole minute with the light on your face, looking at whatever is outside.
> *Too much today?* If it's dark or the window won't open, pull the curtains wide and switch on the brightest light in the room. Then the telephone.
>
> **What I noticed:** ______

The `[ ]` above is only a placeholder in this spec. In the PDF it is a drawn bordered cell.

**What the sample locks:**

- **Voice:** quick bursts, then pauses, teasing (Giulia, Concetta, herself), one uselessly specific detail, a blunt turn ("Listen to me"), and at most one or two Italian words per page.
- **Weight:** about 130 to 190 words of story, a task of one or two sentences, and a smaller version.
- **Structure:** fixed per page, as listed above.
- **Facts:** no fact is stated as hers. When a fact appears, Giulia "read it" or "researchers keep finding", and it lives on the Why page, not on the day pages.

## Safety decisions

- **Health is habits only.** No cures, no "fixes", no dosages, no diet prescriptions. Week Three is about *how* to eat (sitting, no phone, lunch as a meal, cooking simply, eating with people), never *what*.
- **Doctor notes:**
  - Day 21 and the fit page say that a doctor's instructions about eating, drinking or moving always win.
  - The fit page also carries the "go at your own pace / check with your doctor if you have a condition" note.
- **Movement:**
  - Gentle and safe for any body.
  - Every movement day has a smaller or seated version.
  - Stairs are taken with a hand on the rail.
  - Balance work keeps both hands on the counter.
  - Sit-to-stand uses a sturdy chair, and hands are allowed.
  - Pain means stop.
- **Sea and cold water:**
  - Never the buyer's task.
  - Day 5 says plainly "the sea is my story, not your homework."
  - The swap is cool tap water, not ice.
- **Kitchen cards:**
  - Home recipes only.
  - Fish cooked until it flakes.
  - No foraging (wild fennel becomes bought fennel fronds).
  - Leftovers covered and refrigerated.
  - A hot-moka warning.
  - Allergies and doctor's orders come first.
- **Rest:** riposo is rest, not a health claim.
- **Serious cases:** 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988, US), Samaritans 116 123 (UK and Ireland), a local emergency number elsewhere, and a doctor for tiredness that doesn't lift.
- **AI disclosure:** stated plainly on the small-print page: Rosa, Giulia, Concetta and the town are AI-created fiction, and the habits are ordinary and old.
- **No real brands.** The only named organizations are the free help lines.
- **Text hygiene:**
  - No em or en dashes, no unicode arrows, ticks or boxes.
  - The build script greps the source and refuses to build if any appear.

## Production and rebuild

- **Content:**
  - `build/days.js`: the 30 days
  - `build/front.js`: the front matter, week openers and midpoint letter
  - `build/back.js`: the ending, bonuses and small print
- **Templates and style:**
  - `build/templates.js`
  - `build/style.css`
  - `build/book.js`: the page order and contents groups
- **Build commands**, run from `build/` with `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers`:
  1. `node build.js` renders the PDF to `../The-30-Day-Morning-Reset.pdf` and reports overflow and fill for every page. It refuses to build if an em dash, en dash, arrow, tick or box glyph is in the text.
  2. `python3 verify-toc.py` (run from `product/`) checks every contents entry and every printed page reference against the real PDF text.
  3. `node images.js` renders `cover.png` and the two store images from the final book HTML.
